Magnet versus Waratah.

Small town, big rivalry

Waratah was the larger neighbour, but when the riflemen, cricketers and tennis players met, Magnet was perfectly capable of giving it a contest. The two towns shared a tramway connection, visitors, dances and musicians — and a lively sporting relationship.

The old results give us something particularly enjoyable: a chance to put the names and scores back on the board.

On the rifle range

If there was one field on which Magnet enjoyed getting the better of Waratah, the recorded rifle matches make a persuasive case. Waratah edged the April 1911 encounter by just three points. In the following recorded contests, Magnet turned the tables decisively.1

There were some accomplished marksmen in Magnet’s ranks. In the 1911 Boag’s Trophy results, W. Lewis finished on 412 points, R. James on 411, H. Clark on 409 and E. A. de Latour on 408. Four competitors separated by only four points — enough to keep anyone interested until the final shot.1

James Doody was another familiar name on the range. In a July 1915 fixture against Waratah, he scored 53 points. Within weeks, Doody was being farewelled from Magnet before leaving for military training.2

Cricket: twenty-five runs — and then just one

Magnet’s cricketers could also hold their own. A recorded 1925 match finished with Magnet on 124 and Waratah on 99: a twenty-five-run victory to the smaller town.1

Four years later, the schoolboys served up something even closer. Waratah made 121 and Magnet 120. Just one run separated them. It is hard to imagine a result better suited to being argued about over afternoon tea.

There was a bigger achievement as well. Magnet Cricket Club won the 1932–33 Waratah Association premiership, a fine distinction for the town’s sporting record.3

Tennis: rivalry with afternoon tea

The tennis players had their own close encounters. In March 1922, the Waratah team travelled to Magnet by rail motor. Each side won three sets, but Waratah prevailed by 41 games to 38. Magnet provided afternoon tea afterwards, with H. S. Gaunt speaking for the hosts.4

The 1927 result was another Waratah win, this time by seven sets to five and 60 games to 53. That victory levelled the season’s meetings between the clubs, and a deciding match at Waratah was proposed.

Among the names appearing in the tennis records are A. G. Daly, H. G. Valentine, H. S. Gaunt, F. Larkin, F. Johnson, W. Fraser, R. Fraser, B. Fraser, W. Macfarlane, I. Godwin and T. Godwin.

And when an opportunity arose to face another district, the neighbours could become teammates: in 1919, a combined Waratah–Magnet side defeated Ulverstone. Captain Clive Archer, a returned Anzac, was among the players.4

The brass bands join the contest

Sport was only part of the relationship. In February 1909, the Waratah Brass Band visited Magnet with about a hundred excursionists. By October that year Magnet had established a band of its own, with around twenty members.5

Magnet’s players soon made their mark. The band celebrated success at the Burnie competition in January 1911; a later account credited it with first place in the B-grade selection and second in the quickstep. A 1913 contest brought a third placing, with J. Long recording 81 points.6

There was cooperation as well as competition. In December 1917, most of Magnet’s bandsmen joined Waratah’s contest contingent. A decade later, the Waratah Brass Band was back in Magnet for a fundraising ball.7

Rival towns, shared occasions

People crossed between Magnet and Waratah for much more than matches. Magnet’s bachelors’ plain and fancy-dress ball in 1909 attracted about forty couples, including visitors from Waratah. H. L. Ward served as secretary, C. Briscoe as master of ceremonies, while H. Collins and W. Fagan supplied piano and cornet music.8

The two towns were closely connected, but Magnet had its own clubs, musicians, sporting grounds and community pride. The rifle scores, the one-run school cricket match and those closely fought tennis sets give the relationship a human scale.

And on the evidence of the recorded sporting results, the smaller town was quite prepared to take on its larger neighbour — and win.
