A brief chronology

Magnet at a glance.

A few markers in the life of a remarkable west coast mining town. The forthcoming book will explore the people, places and stories in much greater detail.

This short guide is an introduction rather than a substitute for the book. Dates and episodes will be expanded as research continues.

  1. Early 1890s

    A mining field emerges.

    Prospectors and mining companies began to turn a remote part of Tasmania’s west coast into the Magnet field.

  2. 1904

    A community takes shape.

    A public hall and schoolroom were established, reflecting the determination to make a settled town as well as a mining camp.

  3. 1909–1913

    Town life grows.

    Church, social and community activities became part of everyday life as Magnet’s population and public life developed.

  4. 1914–1918

    War touches Magnet.

    Like communities across Tasmania, Magnet felt the impact of the First World War through enlistments, absence and loss.

  5. 1930–1932

    Depression and determination.

    Mine closures and difficult conditions placed the community under severe pressure, while local people continued to seek a future for the field.

  6. 1936–1938

    One more revival.

    Renewed activity brought fresh hopes, but the town’s future remained uncertain.

  7. 1940

    The industrial town is dismantled.

    With mining finished, equipment and buildings were sold or removed, marking the end of Magnet as a working mining town.

The fuller story is still being assembled.
