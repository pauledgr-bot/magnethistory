Research source 02

Historic newspapers.

Newspapers are central to the research for Magnet. They provide dates, names, events and the everyday detail that helps reconstruct the life of a remote Tasmanian mining community.

Trove & the record

Reading Magnet in print.

The project draws on digitised historical newspapers available through Trove, the National Library of Australia’s searchable archive. Reports, notices, advertisements, shipping news and personal items all help trace the people and places connected with Magnet.

The research has drawn on more than 1,000 newspaper records. Each is treated as a lead to be checked against other sources, rather than as the final word on the past.

From the newspapers

Magnet in the newspapers.

Three contemporary reports reveal different sides of life at Magnet: a community building its own school, the bitter conflict at the mine, and the loss of a familiar landmark.

Community · August 1904
1904

A school for Magnet

Residents built a hall and schoolroom with voluntary labour, and celebrated its opening with a concert and dance. Miss Crawford was expected to commence teaching soon afterwards.

Contemporary newspaper report · 3 August 1904

Industrial life · September 1908
1908

The miners’ strike

During the long-running dispute, company chairman W. F. Petterd claimed that 42 strikers remained at Magnet and declared there was no prospect of their re-employment. The report records the company's position, not an independent count.

North Western Advocate and the Emu Bay Times · 5 September 1908 · p. 5
View original article and scan on Trove ↗
Town life · May 1930
1930

The Magnet Hotel fire

In May 1930, fire destroyed the Magnet Hotel, taking away a long-standing meeting place for the district. The story captures one of the losses that marked Magnet's later years.

Advocate · 19 May 1930 · p. 6 · “Magnet Blaze”
